Sources

Every number on this dashboard comes from the sources below. Data was pulled on November 14, 2026.

  • NHL public stats API (api-web.nhle.com) is the source for all NHL games, goals, assists and points by player and season, for player profiles (draft year, round and pick, birth date, college seasons, Stanley Cups, NHL trophies), for the 2026-27 to-date stats and for the league-wide totals used in every share-of-NHL figure. It is an unofficial, undocumented interface, and the data belongs to the NHL.
  • Hobey Baker Memorial Award published winners list is the source for Hobey winners, matched to players by name and school. Of the 46 winners from 1981 to 2026, 39 played in the NHL and are tagged. The other six never played an NHL game (Scott Fusco 1986, Lane MacDonald 1989, Marty Sertich 2005, Ryan Duncan 2007, Jack Connolly 2012, Dryden McKay 2022), and the 2026 winner, Max Plante, is still in college.
  • Compiled by hand for this dashboard: each school's current (2026-27) conference, and each program's first Division I season. For six programs the start year (and, for defunct programs, the last season) was taken from Wikipedia and College Hockey Historian and is approximate: Alabama-Huntsville 1986-87, American International 1996-97 (closed after 2023-24), Canisius 1998-99 (closed after 2023-24), Illinois-Chicago 1981-82 (closed after 1995-96), Mercyhurst 1999-2000 (closed after 2024-25) and Saint Louis 1970-71 (closed after 1977-78). Sources disagree by a year for American International and Mercyhurst.
  • Used only to cross-check, not as a data source: Wikipedia's "Former NCAA players in the National Hockey League" table (link), compared school by school (see How we checked it below).

Calculated by this dashboard, not taken from a source: alumni credit to a last school, decade and season totals, share of NHL, points per game, alumni per 10 program-years, age at debut and years from college to the NHL.

What this is

An interactive view of how NCAA Division I college hockey alumni have performed in the NHL, from the 1960s to today, for fans, program boosters, recruiters and anyone comparing programs.

  • Who: 1,953 alumni from 59 Division I programs, credited to the last college on their NHL profile (a transfer counts only for the final school).
  • What: NHL regular-season games, goals, assists and points, plus draft position, Stanley Cups, Hobey Baker wins and NHL trophies.
  • When: "1960+" in the title means NHL seasons from 1960-61 onward only. Games played before 1960-61 are not counted, so college players of earlier eras (for example Gerry Geran, Dartmouth, in 1917-18) are not included. The data run 1960-61 through 2026-27 to date (November 14, 2026), in seven decades. The 2020s include the partial 2026-27 season. The WHA is not included.
  • Source: the NHL public stats API, pulled November 14, 2026. The 2026-27 season is still in progress; this copy is a snapshot as of that date.

Why this is different from what is already public

College hockey alumni in the NHL are well covered in pieces. None of the public sources we found is built for the questions this dashboard answers:

  • College Hockey Inc. publishes yearly headcounts (how many alumni played, which schools had the most) and a roster matchup tool. It counts players; it does not total their production, compare decades or show share of the league.
  • Wikipedia has one static table per school with players, most games and Cups. No points, no time dimension, no filters.
  • The Hockey News and The Hockey Writers run editorial rankings and best-player features: one era or one list at a time, not something you can re-slice.
  • Elite Prospects tracks college and pro careers player by player, not alumni output rolled up by school.

Use cases built here that none of them address:

  • Career NHL points, goals, assists and games by school or conference, filtered by decade, position and conference.
  • College alumni as a share of all NHL games, goals, assists and points, by decade and by season.
  • Fair comparisons across programs of different ages (alumni per 10 program-years).
  • Development paths by school: draft status, age at NHL debut, years from college to the NHL, Cups and trophies.
  • A view of 2026-27 alumni, by school and NHL team (snapshot as of November 14, 2026).
  • Click-through from any chart or table to a player career, and Excel export of everything behind the screen.

Based on a search of public sources on November 14, 2026. A source we did not find may exist.

How we checked it

We compared the number of alumni per school with Wikipedia's table (July 2025 snapshot). Across the 56 programs it lists with alumni, 14 match exactly, 38 are within one player and 51 are within three.

We also re-scanned the NHL profiles of all 7,391 players who have played in the NHL since 1960-61 (November 14, 2026). The NHL tags college seasons with many different league labels, and five alumni whose seasons carry the Atlantic Hockey tag had been missed: Chris Tanev (RIT), Dave Forbes (American International), Jamie Hunt (Mercyhurst), Cory Conacher (Canisius) and Cole Schneider (UConn). They are now included. A player whose NHL profile shows no college season at all cannot be found this way. The 15 alumni who made their NHL debut in 2026-27 were found from this season's rosters.

Hobey Baker winners were checked against the award's winners list: the 39 winners who reached the NHL are all tagged, and the missing years (1986, 1989, 2005, 2007, 2012, 2022) are winners who never played an NHL game.

Every alumnus's Stanley Cup count was checked against the NHL's own records (1,914 players compared, no differences), and a spot-check of all profiles found no duplicates.

Remaining differences from Wikipedia come mainly from method. This dashboard credits each player to his last school only, while Wikipedia can list a player at several schools. Five schools still differ by four or more players (Alaska Anchorage, Boston University, Denver, Harvard and Union). Wikipedia's Stanley Cup totals by school also differ for several programs (Denver: 10 here, 16 there). Because the Cup counts here match the NHL's records player by player, we believe the gap is Wikipedia's counting method, but we could not confirm what it is.

Not covered

  • Division III, Division II and Canadian university (U Sports) alumni. This is a deliberate scope choice: the dashboard is about NCAA Division I.
  • All-Star selections (no complete public source).
  • Playoff statistics, and players who started in college but never played an NHL game.

                Head to head

                Uses the decade and position filters; the conference filter does not apply. The better value in each row is in bold (for age at debut and years to the NHL, lower is better; program-years is not ranked). Share of the NHL is each school's share of all NHL games or points in the chosen decades and positions. Share drafted, age and years to the NHL are averages over each school's alumni in view, so small groups can swing. Program-years run from the program's first Division I season to its last (or 2026-27).

                Definitions and caveats

                Stats are NHL regular-season games, goals, assists and points. Decades are 1960-61 to 1969-70 (very few alumni; only 23 are tracked), 1970-71 to 1979-80 (NHL only; the WHA is not included), 1980-81 to 1989-90, 1990-91 to 1999-00, 2000-01 to 2009-10 (no 2004-05 season), 2010-11 to 2019-20, and 2020-21 to 2026-27 (seven seasons; 2026-27 is partial, with stats as of November 14, 2026). The decade, position, conference and school menus accept several choices at once; leaving one empty means all.

                Alumni are credited to the last college program on their NHL profile, so a transfer counts only for the final school. Forwards are C, L and R; defensemen are D; goalies are G. Players with no college season on their profile are not alumni here.

                Share of the NHL divides alumni totals by the league-wide totals for every NHL player in the same decade and position group. Alumni means players from the tracked programs only, so players from other colleges are in the league total but not in the numerator, and the shares understate total college impact. A player's NHL totals count all his NHL games in the decade, whenever his college years were.

                Conferences are each school's current (2026-27) alignment, assigned by hand; Notre Dame is Big Ten. Alaska, Alaska Anchorage, Alabama-Huntsville and the now-defunct Canisius, Illinois-Chicago and Saint Louis programs are grouped as Other / unverified. UMass is merged under its several NHL-data spellings; UMass Lowell is separate.

                This dashboard covers NCAA Division I programs only. Players from Division II and III colleges, Canadian universities (U Sports) and club programs are not counted as alumni.

                Development and honors: draft data (year, round, overall pick), birth date and Stanley Cups and individual NHL trophies come from NHL player profiles. Age at debut is age on October 1 of a player's first NHL season. Years from college to NHL is that season's start year minus the start year of his last Division I college season. Hobey Baker winners come from the award's published winners list, matched by name and school. Only winners who played in the NHL are counted (39 of the 46 winners through 2026), so every Hobey count here is NHL players only. All-Star selections are not available from the NHL data and are not included. 500+ game players use a player's career NHL games across all decades shown, regular season only.

                Active now covers 2026-27 regular-season stats as of November 14, 2026. A player's college is taken from his last Division I team in his profile.
